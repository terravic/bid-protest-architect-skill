# GAO & COFC Bid Protest Precedents Handbook

This handbook summarizes core legal standards, statutory citations, and Comptroller General (GAO) and U.S. Court of Federal Claims (COFC) decisions governing federal bid protests under the Federal Acquisition Regulation (FAR) and Title 4 of the Code of Federal Regulations (4 C.F.R. Part 21).

---

## 1. Fundamental Legal Standards

### A. Standard of Review
- **Arbitrary, Capricious, or Violation of Law**: Under the Competition in Contracting Act (CICA) and the Administrative Dispute Resolution Act (ADRA), 28 U.S.C. 1491(b)(1), the GAO and COFC review agency procurement actions to determine whether the agency acted arbitrarily, capriciously, abused its discretion, or acted otherwise not in accordance with procurement law and regulation (5 U.S.C. 706(2)(A)).
- **Review of Technical Evaluations**: The GAO does not re-evaluate proposals *de novo*. Instead, it examines the contemporaneous record to determine whether the evaluation was reasonable, consistent with stated solicitation criteria, and documented in the record (GAO Decision B-416211).

### B. The Mandatory Rule of Competitive Prejudice
- **Prejudice Requirement**: Even where an agency commits an undisputed procedural or evaluation error, a protest will not be sustained unless the protester demonstrates **competitive prejudice** (404 F.3d 1346 (Fed. Cir. 2005); GAO Decision B-412408).
- **Substantial Chance Standard**: To establish competitive prejudice, the protester must demonstrate that, *but for the agency's error, the protester would have had a substantial chance of receiving the contract award*.

### C. Statutory Timeliness Rules (4 C.F.R. 21.2)
1. **Patent Solicitation Defects**: Must be filed *prior to the time set for receipt of initial proposals* (or the next closing time following an amendment).
2. **Post-Award Protests with Required Debriefing**: Must be filed not later than **five (5) calendar days** after the date on which a required debriefing is concluded to trigger the statutory CICA stay of contract performance (31 U.S.C. 3553(d)(4)), and within **ten (10) calendar days** after debriefing conclusion to be timely at the GAO (4 C.F.R. 21.2(a)(2)).
3. **Other Post-Award Protests**: Must be filed within **ten (10) calendar days** after the basis of protest is known or should have been known.

## 2. Controlling Decisions by Protest Ground

### Ground 1: Unstated Evaluation Criteria
- **FAR Authority**: FAR 15.303(a)(2), FAR 15.305(a). Agencies must evaluate competitive proposals solely on the factors and subfactors specified in the solicitation.
- **GAO Decision B-400760**: An agency may not penalize an offeror for omitting infrastructure or capabilities not reasonably encompassed by the stated solicitation criteria.
- **GAO Decision B-416916**: Assigning a weakness for omitting an undisclosed requirement constitutes an unlawful unstated evaluation criterion.

### Ground 2: Disparate / Unequal Treatment
- **FAR Authority**: FAR 1.102-2(c)(3), FAR 15.305. Contracting officers must treat all prospective contractors fairly and impartially.
- **GAO Decision B-409651**: Agencies must evaluate proposals on an equal basis; differences in evaluation outcomes must stem from genuine differences in proposals rather than unequal evaluator scrutiny.
- **GAO Decision B-311344**: Crediting an awardee for a technical capability while downgrading a protester's indistinguishable capability constitutes improper disparate treatment.

### Ground 3: Waiver or Relaxation of Mandatory Solicitation Terms
- **FAR Authority**: FAR 15.206, FAR 15.305. When an agency's needs change or a mandatory requirement is relaxed, the agency must amend the solicitation and afford all offerors an opportunity to compete on the revised basis.
- **GAO Decision B-400394**: A proposal that fails to satisfy a material mandatory solicitation requirement is unacceptable and may not form the basis for award.
- **GAO Decision B-404153**: An agency has no discretion during evaluation to waive explicit mandatory minimum personnel experience thresholds stated in Section L.

### Ground 4: Flawed Past Performance Evaluation
- **FAR Authority**: FAR 15.305(a)(2). Agencies must evaluate past performance relevance (size, scope, and complexity) consistent with solicitation definitions.
- **GAO Decision B-414223**: Crediting an offeror with relevant past performance based on contracts an order of magnitude below the solicitation's dollar and scope thresholds is unreasonable.

### Ground 5: Defective Best Value Cost-Technical Tradeoff
- **FAR Authority**: FAR 15.308, FAR 15.101-1. Source selection decisions must reflect a comparative qualitative assessment and document the rationale for cost-technical tradeoffs.
- **GAO Decision B-293134**: A source selection authority cannot rely mechanically on flawed adjectival ratings or convert a Best Value Tradeoff solicitation into a lowest-price award.
- **GAO Decision B-415755**: Where non-price factors are stated to be significantly more important than price, the SSA must articulate a substantive qualitative basis if declining to pay a price differential for documented technical superiority.

### Grounds 6-8: Discussions, Organizational Conflicts of Interest, and Cost Realism
- **Misleading / Unequal Discussions (FAR 15.306(d))**: GAO Decision B-414886; GAO Decision B-411005 (agencies conducting discussions must identify deficiencies and significant weaknesses equally).
- **Organizational Conflict of Interest (FAR Subpart 9.5)**: GAO Decision B-254397; GAO Decision B-404033 (contracting officers must investigate and mitigate unequal access to information, biased ground rules, and impaired objectivity).
- **Cost / Price Realism (FAR 15.404-1(d))**: GAO Decision B-413721; GAO Decision B-409079 (where realism evaluation is required by Section M, agencies must evaluate whether understated rates reflect lack of technical understanding or performance risk).
