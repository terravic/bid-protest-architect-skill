# GAO & COFC Bid Protest Precedents Handbook

This handbook provides the core legal standards, statutory citations, and landmark Government Accountability Office (GAO) and U.S. Court of Federal Claims (COFC) decisions governing federal bid protests under the Federal Acquisition Regulation (FAR) and Title 4 of the Code of Federal Regulations (4 C.F.R. Part 21).

---

## 1. Fundamental Legal Standards

### A. Standard of Review
- **Arbitrary, Capricious, or Violation of Law**: Under the Competition in Contracting Act (CICA) and the Administrative Dispute Resolution Act (ADRA), 28 U.S.C. § 1491(b)(1), the GAO and COFC review agency procurement actions to determine whether the agency acted arbitrarily, capriciously, an abuse of discretion, or otherwise not in accordance with procurement law and regulation (5 U.S.C. § 706(2)(A)).
- **Deference to Technical Evaluations**: The GAO does not re-evaluate proposals *de novo*. Instead, it examines the record to determine whether the agency's evaluation was reasonable, consistent with the stated evaluation criteria, and supported by documentary evidence (*Raytheon Co.*, B-416211).

### B. The Mandatory Rule of Competitive Prejudice
- **No Prejudice, No Sustained Protest**: Even where an agency commits an undisputed legal or procedural error, the GAO will not sustain a protest unless the protester demonstrates **competitive prejudice** (*Bannum, Inc. v. United States*, 404 F.3d 1346; *Target Media*, B-412408).
- **Standard**: To establish prejudice, the protester must show that, *but for the agency's error, the protester would have had a substantial chance of receiving the contract award*.

### C. Statutory Timeliness Rules (4 C.F.R. § 21.2)
1. **Defects in the Solicitation**: Must be filed *prior to the time set for receipt of initial proposals* (or amended closing date).
2. **Post-Award Protests with Required Debriefing**: Must be filed not later than **five (5) days** after the date on which the debriefing is concluded to trigger the statutory CICA stay of contract performance (31 U.S.C. § 3553(d)(4)), or within **ten (10) days** after the debriefing date to be timely before the GAO (4 C.F.R. § 21.2(a)(2)).
3. **All Other Protests**: Must be filed within **ten (10) days** after the basis of protest is known or reasonably should have been known.

## 2. Landmark Precedents by Protest Ground

### Ground 1: Unstated Evaluation Criteria
- **FAR Rule**: FAR 15.303(a)(2), FAR 15.305(a). Agencies must evaluate competitive proposals based solely on the factors and subfactors specified in the solicitation.
- **Landmark Case**: *Alion Science & Technology Corp.*, B-400760. Held: An agency may not penalize an offeror for failing to include features or approaches not reasonably encompassed by the stated RFP criteria.
- **Landmark Case**: *Vistronix, LLC*, B-416916. Held: Where the solicitation did not require a specific staffing matrix format, the agency's assignment of a weakness for omitting it was an unstated criterion.

### Ground 2: Disparate / Unequal Treatment
- **FAR Rule**: FAR 1.102-2(c)(3), FAR 15.305. Contracting officers shall treat all prospective contractors fairly and impartially.
- **Landmark Case**: *Raytheon Co.*, B-409651. Held: It is fundamental that agencies must evaluate proposals on an equal basis. If an agency gives a strength to one offeror for a particular feature, it must give a similar strength to another offeror proposing the same feature, or document a rational basis for the distinction.
- **Landmark Case**: *Boeing Co.*, B-311344. Held: The agency engaged in disparate treatment by crediting the awardee for a capability while finding the protester's identical capability unsatisfactory.

### Ground 3: Waiver or Relaxation of Mandatory Solicitation Terms
- **FAR Rule**: FAR 15.206, FAR 15.305. If a requirement is mandatory, an agency cannot waive it for one offeror without amending the solicitation and allowing all offerors to compete on the revised basis.
- **Landmark Case**: *ArmorWorks Enterprises, LLC*, B-400394. Held: A proposal that fails to conform to a material solicitation term is unacceptable and may not form the basis for award.
- **Landmark Case**: *Northeast Military Sales*, B-404153. Held: The agency improperly waived mandatory personnel experience minimums for the awardee while enforcing them against others.

### Ground 4: Flawed Best Value / Cost-Technical Tradeoff
- **FAR Rule**: FAR 15.308. The source selection decision must be based on a comparative assessment of proposals and must document the rationale for any business judgments and tradeoffs made.
- **Landmark Case**: *Blue Rock Structures, Inc.*, B-293134. Held: A source selection authority cannot merely rely on adjectival ratings as a mechanical substitute for qualitative tradeoff analysis.
- **Landmark Case**: *General Dynamics Information Technology, Inc.*, B-415755. Held: The SSA failed to explain why the awardee's lower price outweighed the protester's higher technical merit where technical was significantly more important than price.

### Ground 5: Flawed Past Performance Evaluation
- **FAR Rule**: FAR 15.305(a)(2). Agencies must evaluate past performance based on relevance (size, scope, complexity) and quality.
- **Landmark Case**: *Fluor Federal Solutions, LLC*, B-414223. Held: The agency unreasonably credited an offeror with relevant past performance where the contract references were orders of magnitude smaller in dollar value and scope.
