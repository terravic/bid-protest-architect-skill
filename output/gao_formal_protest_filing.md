Apex Federal Solutions, LLC ("Apex" or "Protester"), by and through its designated counsel, Jane Doe, Esq. of Sterling, Vance & Associates, LLP, hereby files this formal Bid Protest pursuant to 31 U.S.C. §§ 3551-3556 and 4 C.F.R. ### I. JURISDICTION, TIMELINESS, AND CICA AUTOMATIC STAY
1. **Jurisdiction**: The Comptroller General has jurisdiction over this procurement pursuant to the Competition in Contracting Act of 1984 ("CICA"), 31 U.S.C. § 3551 *et seq.*

Protester submitted timely debriefing questions (2026-08-12), and the Agency concluded the required debriefing on August 14, 2026. Under 4 C.F.R. § 21.2(a)(2) and 31 U.S.C. § 3553(d)(4), filing on or before August 19, 2026 (5:00 PM EST) triggers the mandatory statutory stay of contract performance.
3. **Interested Party Standing**: Protester is an "interested party" under 4 C.F.R. § 21.0(a)(1) as an actual offeror whose direct economic interest is affected by the award and who stands next in line for award under a lawful evaluation.

### II. STATEMENT OF FACTS
1. The Agency issued Solicitation No. HHS-CMS-2026-CLOUD-009 (2026-04-15) for Federal Healthcare Infrastructure Security & Cloud Modernization (FHIS-CM) as a Firm-Fixed-Price procurement.
2. Section M specified an evaluation basis of Best Value Tradeoff (FAR 15.101-1) (Non-price factors combined are significantly more important than Price).
3. Apex Federal Solutions, LLC submitted a proposal evaluated at $94,500,000.00.
4. The Agency selected Nexus Defense Systems, Inc. for award at an evaluated price of $82,200,000.00.

### III. GROUNDS FOR PROTEST

#### COUNT I: UNSTATED EVALUATION CRITERIA IN FACTOR 1 (TECHNICAL & CLOUD ARCHITECTURE APPROACH) (FAR 15.303(a)(2), FAR 15.305(a))
1. *Applicable Legal Standard*: Under FAR 15.303(a)(2), FAR 15.305(a) and established GAO precedent (*Alion Science & Technology Corp., B-400760; Vistronix, LLC, B-416916*), the Agency is legally required to evaluate proposals strictly in accordance with the stated terms of the solicitation and treat all offerors equally.
2. *Agency Error & Contemporaneous Record*: RFP Section M and Section L set forth the Technical & Cloud Architecture Approach evaluation requirements without mentioning or requiring 'dedicated on-premises physical datacenter hardware bridge facility to connect with legacy Baltimore CMS mainframe hardware.' The TET assigned a 'Significant Weakness' to Protester (Apex) on the ground that Apex did not propose 'dedicated on-premises physical datacenter hardware bridge facility to connect with legacy Baltimore CMS mainframe hardware.' Neither Section L nor Section M mentions or requires this feature. The Agency penalized Protester based on an undisclosed criterion that was not reasonably encompassed by the stated evaluation factors in Section M.
3. *Competitive Prejudice*: The contemporaneous evaluation record confirms this Significant Weakness caused Apex's Factor 1 rating degradation from Outstanding to Acceptable. Removing this unlawful weakness elevates Apex to Outstanding under the most heavily weighted evaluation factor.
4. *Rebuttal to Discretion Claims*: Anticipated Agency Defense: The agency will argue that the unstated feature is 'logically related' to the general technical scope. Rebuttal: GAO precedent strictly prohibits agencies from introducing material infrastructure or architectural mandates under general technical criteria without notice in Section M (Alion Sci. & Tech.).

#### COUNT II: IMPROPER WAIVER OF MANDATORY KEY PERSONNEL QUALIFICATIONS FOR AWARDEE (FACTOR 2) (FAR 15.206, FAR 15.305)
1. *Applicable Legal Standard*: Under FAR 15.206, FAR 15.305 and established GAO precedent (*ArmorWorks Enterprises, LLC, B-400394; Northeast Military Sales, B-404153*), the Agency is legally required to evaluate proposals strictly in accordance with the stated terms of the solicitation and treat all offerors equally.
2. *Agency Error & Contemporaneous Record*: RFP Section L.4 explicitly mandated that Lead Cloud Solutions Architect possess a minimum of 10 years of direct enterprise experience and required professional/security certifications, warning that failure to satisfy mandatory minimums would result in an 'Unacceptable' rating. Nexus proposed Johnathan Brooks, who possessed only 4 years of experience and lacked mandatory certifications, yet the TET waived the deficiency and awarded Nexus an 'Acceptable' rating. The Agency waived a mandatory minimum solicitation requirement for the Awardee without amending the RFP or affording Protester the same opportunity to propose relaxed staffing.
3. *Competitive Prejudice*: Under FAR 15.206 and established GAO rule, a proposal that fails to meet a mandatory minimum qualification is non-compliant and ineligible for award. Nexus should have been eliminated from the competition.
4. *Rebuttal to Discretion Claims*: Anticipated Agency Defense: The agency has discretion in evaluating candidate qualifications. Rebuttal: Where an RFP specifies mandatory minimum years of experience and certifications ('shall possess minimum 10 years'), the agency has no legal discretion to waive the requirement post-submission (Northeast Military Sales).

#### COUNT III: ACCEPTANCE OF TECHNICALLY NON-COMPLIANT AVAILABILITY SLA FROM AWARDEE (FACTOR 1) (FAR 15.206, FAR 15.305)
1. *Applicable Legal Standard*: Under FAR 15.206, FAR 15.305 and established GAO precedent (*Raytheon Co., B-416211; ArmorWorks Enterprises, LLC, B-400394*), the Agency is legally required to evaluate proposals strictly in accordance with the stated terms of the solicitation and treat all offerors equally.
2. *Agency Error & Contemporaneous Record*: RFP Section L.3.1 established a mandatory minimum availability SLA threshold of 99.99% monthly uptime, explicitly stating that proposals offering below 99.99% 'will be deemed non-compliant and ineligible for award.' Nexus expressly proposed a 99.95% monthly uptime SLA. The TET failed to assign a deficiency and awarded Nexus a 'Good' rating. The Agency awarded the contract to an offeror whose proposal took explicit exception to a material performance threshold.
3. *Competitive Prejudice*: Nexus's proposal was facially non-compliant with a mandatory solicitation term and should have been rejected as ineligible.
4. *Rebuttal to Discretion Claims*: Anticipated Agency Defense: 99.95% was deemed functionally adequate during evaluation. Rebuttal: An agency cannot unilaterally relax a mandatory RFP performance requirement during evaluation without amending the solicitation (Raytheon Co.).

#### COUNT IV: UNREASONABLE PAST PERFORMANCE EVALUATION OF AWARDEE (FACTOR 3) (FAR 15.305(a)(2))
1. *Applicable Legal Standard*: Under FAR 15.305(a)(2) and established GAO precedent (*Fluor Federal Solutions, LLC, B-414223; Metro Machine Corp., B-402567*), the Agency is legally required to evaluate proposals strictly in accordance with the stated terms of the solicitation and treat all offerors equally.
2. *Agency Error & Contemporaneous Record*: RFP Section L.5 and Section M defined 'relevant' past performance as enterprise federal contracts with value >= $50,000,000 and matching regulatory/mission scope. Nexus submitted non-relevant commercial/state references ($3.20M, $8.50M), yet the TET assigned Nexus a 'Satisfactory Confidence (Very Good)' rating. The Agency failed to evaluate past performance in accordance with the solicitation's stated criteria for size, scope, and complexity.
3. *Competitive Prejudice*: Proper evaluation under Section M relevance thresholds would have yielded Limited or Neutral Confidence for Nexus, widening Apex's substantial non-price superiority (Apex held Substantial Confidence including a $91.20M relevant contract).
4. *Rebuttal to Discretion Claims*: Anticipated Agency Defense: Evaluators possess broad latitude in assessing past performance relevance. Rebuttal: Assigning a favorable confidence rating to commercial references an order of magnitude below the stated RFP dollar floor violates FAR 15.305(a)(2) (Fluor Fed. Sols.).

#### COUNT V: DEFECTIVE BEST VALUE COST-TECHNICAL TRADEOFF DECISION (FAR 15.308) (FAR 15.308, FAR 15.101-1)
1. *Applicable Legal Standard*: Under FAR 15.308, FAR 15.101-1 and established GAO precedent (*Blue Rock Structures, Inc., B-293134; General Dynamics IT, Inc., B-415755*), the Agency is legally required to evaluate proposals strictly in accordance with the stated terms of the solicitation and treat all offerors equally.
2. *Agency Error & Contemporaneous Record*: Section M.1 expressly established that Non-Price Factors combined were 'non-price factors combined are significantly more important than price.' The SSA's Source Selection Decision Document mechanically relied on Nexus's lower price ($82.20M vs. $94.50M) and flawed underlying technical ratings without conducting a valid qualitative tradeoff explaining why Apex's non-price superiority (Key Personnel and Management Plan and Past Performance) did not warrant the price delta. The SSA tainted the source selection with defective underlying evaluations and converted a Best Value Tradeoff procurement into an improper Lowest Price Technically Acceptable (LPTA) award.
3. *Competitive Prejudice*: Because Apex was technically superior across non-price factors under a compliant evaluation and non-price factors significantly outweighed price, Apex had a substantial chance of receiving award under a lawful tradeoff.
4. *Rebuttal to Discretion Claims*: Anticipated Agency Defense: The SSA exercises broad business judgment in cost-technical tradeoffs. Rebuttal: Broad discretion cannot salvage a tradeoff decision predicated on unlawful underlying evaluation errors or devoid of qualitative rationale (Blue Rock Structures).
