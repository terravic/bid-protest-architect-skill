## FORMAL BID PROTEST

Apex Federal Solutions, LLC ("Apex" or "Protester"), by and through its designated counsel, Jane Doe, Esq. of Sterling, Vance & Associates, LLP, hereby files this formal Bid Protest pursuant to 31 U.S.C. §§ 3551-3556 and 4 C.F.R. Part 21 challenging the contract award under Request for Proposals ("RFP") No. HHS-CMS-2026-CLOUD-009 to Nexus Defense Systems, Inc. ("Nexus" or "Awardee").

### I. JURISDICTION, TIMELINESS, AND CICA AUTOMATIC STAY
1. **Jurisdiction**: The Comptroller General has jurisdiction over this procurement pursuant to the Competition in Contracting Act of 1984 ("CICA"), 31 U.S.C. § 3551 *et seq.*
2. **Timeliness**: Protester received a written debriefing on August 10, 2026. Protester submitted timely debriefing questions on August 12, 2026, and the Agency delivered written responses on August 14, 2026. Under 4 C.F.R. § 21.2(a)(2) and 31 U.S.C. § 3553(d)(4), this protest is filed within five (5) days of the conclusion of the required debriefing, thereby triggering the mandatory statutory stay of contract performance.
3. **Interested Party Standing**: Protester is an "interested party" under 4 C.F.R. § 21.0(a)(1) as an actual offeror whose direct economic interest is affected by the award. Protester submitted an exceptional technical proposal and is next in line for contract award.

### II. STATEMENT OF FACTS
1. On April 15, 2026, CMS issued RFP No. HHS-CMS-2026-CLOUD-009 seeking enterprise cloud modernization services.
2. Section M.1 specified that award would be made on a Best Value Tradeoff basis where Non-Price Factors (Factors 1, 2, and 3) combined were *significantly more important than Price*.
3. Factor 1 (Technical) was designated the most important factor, followed by Factor 2 (Key Personnel) and Factor 3 (Past Performance).
4. Protester submitted a fully compliant proposal priced at $94,500,000.00 offering a 99.999% SLA and 14-year veteran key personnel.
5. Awardee submitted a proposal priced at $82,200,000.00 offering a non-compliant 99.95% SLA and an under-qualified Lead Architect.

### III. GROUNDS FOR PROTEST

#### COUNT I: UNSTATED EVALUATION CRITERIA IN FACTOR 1 (TECHNICAL & CLOUD ARCHITECTURE) (FAR 15.303(a)(2), FAR 15.305(a))
1. *Applicable Legal Standard*: Under FAR 15.303(a)(2), FAR 15.305(a) and established GAO precedent (*Alion Science & Technology Corp., B-400760; Vistronix, LLC, B-416916*), the Agency is legally required to evaluate proposals strictly in accordance with the stated terms of the solicitation and treat all offerors equally.
2. *Agency Error & Contemporaneous Record*: RFP Section M.1 and L.3 set forth the cloud architecture evaluation requirements, focusing on FedRAMP High multi-region deployment and Zero Trust. The TET assigned a 'Significant Weakness' to Protester (Apex) on the sole ground that Apex did not propose an 'on-premises physical datacenter hardware bridge facility for legacy Baltimore CMS mainframe hardware.' Neither Section L nor Section M mentions or requires any on-premises physical datacenter hardware bridge. The Agency penalized Protester based on an undisclosed criterion that was not reasonably encompassed by the stated evaluation factors in Section M.
3. *Competitive Prejudice*: The TET explicitly noted that this single Significant Weakness was the sole reason Apex's Factor 1 rating was reduced from Outstanding to Acceptable. Removing this unlawful weakness elevates Apex to Outstanding under the most heavily weighted evaluation factor.
4. *Rebuttal to Discretion Claims*: Anticipated Agency Defense: The agency will argue that on-premise mainframe connectivity is 'logically related' to healthcare systems. Rebuttal: GAO precedent strictly prohibits agencies from introducing major infrastructure mandates under general technical criteria without prior notice in Section M (Alion Sci. & Tech.).

#### COUNT II: IMPROPER WAIVER OF MANDATORY KEY PERSONNEL QUALIFICATIONS FOR AWARDEE (FACTOR 2) (FAR 15.206, FAR 15.305)
1. *Applicable Legal Standard*: Under FAR 15.206, FAR 15.305 and established GAO precedent (*ArmorWorks Enterprises, LLC, B-400394; Northeast Military Sales, B-404153*), the Agency is legally required to evaluate proposals strictly in accordance with the stated terms of the solicitation and treat all offerors equally.
2. *Agency Error & Contemporaneous Record*: RFP Section L.4 explicitly mandated that Key Personnel Position 1 (Lead Cloud Solutions Architect) 'shall possess a minimum of ten (10) years of direct experience architecting enterprise federal cloud solutions' and maintain active AWS Professional / Google Cloud Fellow and CISSP certifications. Section L.4 warned that failure to meet mandatory minimums would result in an 'Unacceptable' rating. Nexus proposed Johnathan Brooks, who possessed only 4 years of total cloud experience and lacked the mandatory certifications. The TET explicitly acknowledged this deficiency but waived it by stating 'commercial aptitude compensates for the shortage,' awarding Nexus an 'Acceptable' rating. The Agency waived a mandatory minimum solicitation requirement for the Awardee without amending the RFP or affording Protester the same opportunity to propose alternative staffing.
3. *Competitive Prejudice*: Under FAR 15.206 and established GAO rule, a proposal that fails to meet a mandatory minimum qualification is non-compliant and ineligible for award. Nexus should have been eliminated from the competition.
4. *Rebuttal to Discretion Claims*: Anticipated Agency Defense: The agency has discretion in evaluating candidate qualifications. Rebuttal: Where an RFP specifies mandatory minimum years of experience and certifications ('shall possess minimum 10 years'), the agency has no legal discretion to waive the requirement post-submission (Northeast Military Sales).

#### COUNT III: ACCEPTANCE OF TECHNICALLY NON-COMPLIANT AVAILABILITY SLA FROM AWARDEE (FACTOR 1) (FAR 15.206, FAR 15.305)
1. *Applicable Legal Standard*: Under FAR 15.206, FAR 15.305 and established GAO precedent (*Raytheon Co., B-416211; ArmorWorks Enterprises, LLC, B-400394*), the Agency is legally required to evaluate proposals strictly in accordance with the stated terms of the solicitation and treat all offerors equally.
2. *Agency Error & Contemporaneous Record*: RFP Section L.3.1 established a mandatory minimum availability SLA threshold of 99.99% monthly uptime, explicitly stating that proposals offering below 99.99% 'will be deemed non-compliant and ineligible for award.' Nexus expressly proposed a 99.95% monthly uptime SLA. The TET failed to assign a deficiency and awarded Nexus a 'Good' rating. The Agency awarded the contract to an offeror whose proposal took explicit exception to a material performance threshold.
3. *Competitive Prejudice*: Nexus's proposal was facially non-compliant with a mandatory term and should have been rejected.
4. *Rebuttal to Discretion Claims*: Anticipated Agency Defense: 99.95% was deemed functionally adequate during evaluation. Rebuttal: An agency cannot unilaterally relax a mandatory RFP performance requirement during evaluation (Raytheon Co.).

#### COUNT IV: UNREASONABLE PAST PERFORMANCE EVALUATION OF AWARDEE (FACTOR 3) (FAR 15.305(a)(2))
1. *Applicable Legal Standard*: Under FAR 15.305(a)(2) and established GAO precedent (*Fluor Federal Solutions, LLC, B-414223; Metro Machine Corp., B-402567*), the Agency is legally required to evaluate proposals strictly in accordance with the stated terms of the solicitation and treat all offerors equally.
2. *Agency Error & Contemporaneous Record*: RFP Section L.5 and M defined 'relevant' past performance as enterprise federal cloud engineering contracts with total value >= $50,000,000, multi-region GovCloud, and FedRAMP High healthcare scope. Nexus submitted two commercial retail/state references ($3.2M and $8.5M value). The TET nonetheless assigned Nexus a 'Satisfactory Confidence (Very Good)' rating. The Agency failed to evaluate past performance in accordance with the stated criteria for size, scope, and complexity.
3. *Competitive Prejudice*: Proper evaluation under Section M relevance criteria would have resulted in a Limited Confidence or Neutral rating for Nexus, widening Apex's overwhelming non-price superiority (Apex had Substantial Confidence on a $91M incumbent contract).
4. *Rebuttal to Discretion Claims*: Anticipated Agency Defense: Evaluators have wide latitude to assess relevance. Rebuttal: Assigning a favorable confidence rating based on commercial work 15x smaller than the RFP threshold violates FAR 15.305 (Fluor Fed. Sols.).

#### COUNT V: DEFECTIVE BEST VALUE COST-TECHNICAL TRADEOFF DECISION (FAR 15.308) (FAR 15.308, FAR 15.101-1)
1. *Applicable Legal Standard*: Under FAR 15.308, FAR 15.101-1 and established GAO precedent (*Blue Rock Structures, Inc., B-293134; General Dynamics IT, Inc., B-415755*), the Agency is legally required to evaluate proposals strictly in accordance with the stated terms of the solicitation and treat all offerors equally.
2. *Agency Error & Contemporaneous Record*: Section M.1 expressly provided that Non-Price Factors (Factors 1, 2, and 3) combined were 'significantly more important than Factor 4 (Price).' The SSA's Source Selection Decision Document mechanically relied on Nexus's lower price ($82.2M vs $94.5M) and an unlawfully contaminated Factor 1 rating without conducting a substantive qualitative tradeoff or explaining why Apex's superior Key Personnel (Outstanding vs Acceptable) and Past Performance (Substantial vs Satisfactory) did not warrant the price difference. The SSA converted a Best Value Tradeoff procurement into an improper Lowest Price Technically Acceptable (LPTA) award.
3. *Competitive Prejudice*: Because Apex was technically superior under compliant evaluation across all three non-price factors, and non-price factors were significantly more important than price, Apex had a substantial chance of award under a lawful tradeoff.
4. *Rebuttal to Discretion Claims*: Anticipated Agency Defense: The SSA has broad business discretion to decide if technical advantages justify price premium. Rebuttal: Broad discretion does not excuse an SSA from failing to explain the qualitative rationale or relying on flawed underlying ratings (Blue Rock Structures).
